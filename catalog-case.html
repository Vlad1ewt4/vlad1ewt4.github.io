01 / Frame the problem

A queue of products.
A series of decisions.

The starting question: how could a small merchant team improve inconsistent catalog content while keeping control over what becomes a product claim?

The concept focuses on one repeatable job: inspect a product, compare its content with supplied facts, make a correction, and leave a clear review history. Bulk generation and automatic publishing are intentionally outside the scope.

This is an original portfolio exploration, developed with AI assistance for scenario analysis, interface copy and implementation. It is not commissioned work, a released merchant product, or evidence of prior customer results.

Working assumptions

What the design explores

A small operations team owns catalog quality. Interruptions are common, source data can be incomplete, and a draft should never silently become approved content.

Known project facts

What exists today

A runnable browser prototype, a synthetic product dataset and predetermined suggestions. No merchant interviews or production analytics were collected for this concept.

03 / Design decisions

Trust is built
at the decision point.

A

Separate source facts from generated language.

For the Arc ceramic mug, “Stoneware” is supplied information; the rewritten description is a draft. Showing both helps a reviewer judge the proposal. A confidence label alone would offer less useful evidence.

Trade-off: a slightly denser review panel in exchange for a visible basis for approval.

B

Missing information needs a different exit.

The Canvas weekender has no verified composition. “Request information” moves it to a waiting state instead of encouraging a plausible-sounding guess. Review progress should not depend on inventing product facts.

Trade-off: unresolved work remains in the system, but the uncertainty stays explicit.

C

Approval must be editable and recoverable.

“Apply draft” fills editable fields; it does not finish the review. The reviewer must confirm checking the source facts before saving. Retained input after failure, retry and undo make the action boundary visible.

Trade-off: one extra deliberate step rather than one-click automatic acceptance.

04 / Reusable foundations

One vocabulary.
Across every state.

Code-level tokens and shared patterns make this prototype inspectable. This is not a claim of an existing Figma library or a production design system.

Semantic color tokens

Ink#18242D
Canvas#F4F6F3
Accent#B7EF73

Accent signals selection, not success on its own. State labels remain readable without relying on color.

Components with a contract

Review action
Visible label, keyboard focus, required validation and retry.
Product status
Needs review, Reviewed or Waiting for info.
Editable field
Persistent label, required validation and retained input.
Review panel
Desktop drawer; mobile layout prioritizes one product.
↔

Density changes. The task does not.

Desktop supports scanning multiple products. On smaller screens the workflow prioritizes product context and full-width controls, with the same review states and recovery paths.

Specify behavior,
including the edges.

Acceptance criteria for the concept workflow
SituationExpected behavior
No matching productsExplain the empty result and let the reviewer clear the search or change filters.
Required content missingKeep the panel open, identify the invalid field, and preserve all entered content.
Save failsDo not mark the product Reviewed. Keep edits available and offer Retry save.
Review savedUpdate the product state and activity. Offer Undo with an understandable outcome.
Keyboard navigationMake actions reachable, show focus, support Escape in the review panel, and restore focus when it closes.

06 / What to validate next

A working concept.
Not a measured outcome.

The deliverable makes design choices testable. Whether those choices help real merchant teams is still an open question.

Recommended next step: moderated sessions with catalog operations staff, followed by a narrow pilot if the workflow proves useful. Business impact and usability improvements are hypotheses, not reported results.

  1. Review a supported draft.Find Arc ceramic mug. Compare the supplied facts, apply a draft, edit the fields, confirm the source check and save.
  2. Handle an unknown fact.Open Canvas weekender. Request source information for the missing composition instead of inventing it.
  3. Recover from failure.Enable Fail next save, confirm the source check and save. Retry without retyping, then undo the review.
ObserveTask completion + errors
AskCan reviewers explain the source?
